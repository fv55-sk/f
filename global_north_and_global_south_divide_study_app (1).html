Dimensions of the Global North-South Divide

Understand key definitions, structural imbalances, aggregate income distribution, and UN Millennium Development Goals (MDGs).

THE GLOBAL NORTH ("Developed Countries")
  • Geography: North America, Europe, Australia, Japan
  • World Population Share: 1/4 (25%)
  • World Income Share: 4/5 (80%)
  • Manufacturing Owned: 90% of global total

Characterized by established democracy, wealth, technological advancement, political stability, aging population, and zero population growth.

THE GLOBAL SOUTH ("Developing Countries")
  • Geography: Africa, Latin America, Developing Asia
  • World Population Share: 3/4 (75%)
  • World Income Share: 1/5 (20%)
  • Primary Economy: Raw Material, Agriculture, Factory Labor

Refers to interconnected histories of colonialism, neo-imperialism, and differential social changes maintaining unequal living standards.

Mandatory Slide Vocabulary ("Words To Go By")

First World

Industrialized & Developed

Countries that are fully industrialized, technologically advanced, and economically developed.

Third World

Developing & Semi-Industrialized

Countries that are mostly developing, non-industrialized, or semi-industrialized economies.

Neocolonialism

Indirect Domination

Indirect colonialism where a neocolony is dominated economically, culturally, or politically by a powerful country.

Austerity

Policy of cutting state budget for social services (health, education) to reduce a country's budget deficit.

Absolute Poverty

Severe Deprivation

Severe deprivation of basic human needs including food, safe drinking water, sanitation, health, shelter, and education.

Regionalism

Closer Economic Integration

Process of closer economic integration of countries within a region (culturally and geographically linked).

Major Theories of International Relations (Slides 14–20)

Comparing Realism, Liberalism, Marxism, and Constructivism in evaluating global inequality.

REALISM Politics-Centric

Assumes the international system is inherently disorderly and relations are **anarchic**. Primary actors (States) are perpetually locked in a struggle for power and national survival.

Core Premise: Self-interest, relative gains, zero-sum power.
Power Logic: A state either has power or none at all; no middle ground.
LIBERALISM Politics-Centric

Centers on rejecting pure power politics. Believes cooperation, economic interdependence, supranational authorities, and non-state actors (NGOs, TNCs) shape peace.

Core Premise: Free trade, rule-based institutions, absolute gains.
Key Actors: States + Supranational Bodies + Business Organizations.
MARXISM Economics-Centric

Based on Karl Marx’s economic principles. Argues that global instability and inequality stem directly from **global class struggle**, capitalist exploitation, and institutionalized injustice.

4 Causes of Instability: Capitalism, Resource Exploitation by the Few, Uneven Wealth Distribution, Institutionalized Inequality.
CONSTRUCTIVISM Social-Construct

Views international politics as shaped by **identities, shared meanings, and changing social norms**. Conceives states as social beings whose interests are constructed through interaction.

Core Motto: *"Anarchy is what states make of it."* Identities dictate actions.

Pro-Globalist & Western Hegemony Paradigms (Slide 21)

Key theories that justify global free trade, capitalist integration, and Western-centric development models.

Globalism (Manfred Steger) Normative Goal

Global economic integration is not only inevitable due to technology, but is a **normative international goal** that all societies should actively pursue.

Modernization Theory (W.W. Rostow) 5 Stages of Growth

Outlines historical progress based on a society's internal capacity to produce and consume material goods through linear developmental stages.

"End of History" (Francis Fukuyama) Capitalist Telos

The total triumph of Western liberal democracy and free-market capitalism marks the final endpoint (*telos*) of human political evolution.

"The Lexus & Olive Tree" (Thomas Friedman) Global Imperative

Presents progress as a binary choice: either embrace global free trade (the *Lexus*) or face economic stagnation and irrelevance (the alternative).

"Clash of Civilizations" (Samuel Huntington) Cultural Conflict

Argues that primary conflicts in the post-Cold War era will not be economic or ideological, but cultural and religious between distinct global civilizations.

Challenging the Global Order: Southern Resistance (Slide 21 & 22)

How leaders and thinkers of the Global South critique Western economic control.

Vladimir Lenin: Imperialism as the Highest Stage of Capitalism

Marxist Imperialism

Capitalism's survival in core wealthy nations depends directly on invading and securing new foreign markets, extracting cheap labor, and seizing raw materials via **imperialism**.

President Sukarno (Indonesia): Colonialism in Modern Dress

1955 Bandung Spirit
"Colonialism has also its modern dress, in the form of economic control, intellectual control, actual physical control by a small but alien community within a nation. It is a skilful and determined enemy, it appears in many guises..."

Third Worldism & Southern Solidarity

Non-Aligned Movement

Began as a collective resistance by post-colonial nations against new forms of imperialist economic domination, seeking alternative paths to sovereign development.

"The Starbucks and the Shanty" Concept (Slide 13 & 14)

Understanding spatial inequalities within both Northern and Southern borders.

Core Concept: Spatial & Entangled Geographies

Spaces of Affluence in the South

High-end luxury districts, Starbucks cafes, gated communities, and financial towers in cities like Manila, Jakarta, or Mumbai mirror the wealth of the Global North.

Spaces of Underdevelopment in the North

Impoverished urban ghettos, tent cities, and neglected rural areas in wealthy nations (e.g., US or UK inner cities) mirror the poverty of the Global South.

Slide Key Takeaway: "The Global South is everywhere, but it is also somewhere... located at the intersection of entangled political geographies of dispossession and repossession."
